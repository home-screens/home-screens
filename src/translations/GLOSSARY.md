# Home Screens glossary

The words every dictionary uses for the same thing, for anyone writing English keys and for every translator. One line per concept. Translators: keep the distinction each line draws, in the everyday words of your language, and reuse the words your locale already has for it.

- **Home Screens**: the box that runs everything. The phone and the wall always say Home Screens ("Couldn't reach Home Screens", "Check that Home Screens is on"), never "the hub", "the main Pi" or "the device". The editor may say "the hub" only where it sets the box against the other displays it drives or against somewhere else a backup is kept, and "the device" only for the computer itself (its password, its name on the network, restarting the whole device).
- **the wall**: the screens the family looks at, on the phone and on the wall itself ("Show on the wall", "every wall", "Which wall?", "This wall"). **display** is the editor's word for one display it configures (Settings > Displays, "Edit display", "This display's screens"); the phone and the wall do not say it.
- **screen**: one page of the wall's rotation ("Nothing on this screen yet"), not the hardware.
- **grown-up**: the adult in the house ("Ask a grown-up", "For grown-ups", the grown-up code). Never "parent" or "adult".
- **person / people**: someone in the family ("Add person", "3 people", "On each person's card"). Never "member"; "family member" is fine in a sentence.
- **Kids' chores** and **Family remote**: the names of the two phone pages, everywhere they are named.
- **Pictures & videos**: the editor's picture library, its Settings page and everything that opens it (the picture picker, photo sources, backups). In the editor a single file is a "picture". On the phone it is "photos", because that is the tab's name.
- **Wi-Fi**: always this spelling, never "wifi" or "WiFi".
- **recipe link**: the web address of a recipe ("Recipe link"), not "Recipe URL".
- **Tap on the wall**: the one name of the switch that lets the wall react to a tap, on every module that has it.
- **Delete**: gone for good ("Delete chore", "Delete folder", "Delete display").
- **Remove**: taken off something, and the thing itself still exists (a person taken off a chore, an event taken off a calendar, "Remove done items" from a list).
- **Clear**: empty a slot, the slot stays ("Clear week", "Clear alerts").
- **Save**: keep changes to something that exists. A form that makes something new says what it makes ("Add chore", "Add meal", "Add reward", "Add to calendar"); its title says "New x", and editing says "Edit x" with a plain "Save".
- **Back up** (verb) and **backup** (noun): "Back up now", "Back up everything", but "Backup saved", "Restore a backup".
- **Copied**: the one word after something is copied.
- **Sentence case**: only the first word and names take a capital ("Show week numbers", "Accent color"). Names keep theirs: module type names, template names, products (Google Calendar, iCloud, Mealie, Todoist, Home Assistant, OneDrive, Immich, Unsplash), days, months, and a Settings page named in a sentence ("Open Family", "from Settings").
- **…**: one ellipsis character for something still happening ("Saving…") or a choice that opens more ("Custom…"), never three dots. A button that is busy says what it is doing ("Saving…"), never a bare "…".
- **No em-dash** (U+2014) anywhere: write a comma, a colon or a full stop instead.
- **No "+ " in a label**: the button draws a plus icon beside plain words ("Add subject").
- **Full stops**: a full sentence ends with one, in help text, errors and questions' bodies. Fragments (card subtitles, labels, menu lines) and the wall's "set it up in the editor and it shows here" lines take none.
- **Errors** say what happened, then what to do: "That didn't save. Try again."
- **Where to find it**: page names joined with " > " ("Settings > Calendar"), built from the sidebar's own names.
- **Website docs** follow the same words: "the wall" where they describe the phone or the wall, "display" where they describe an editor setting for one display.
